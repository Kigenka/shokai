Write "Shōkai", never "Shōkai®".

The Apache License 2.0 (§6) and CC BY 4.0 (§2(b)(2)) don't grant any rights in names or logos. This page says how you may use them, so that people can trust that something called Shōkai is Shōkai.

## Fine without asking

- **Describing what you built, accurately:** "implements Shōkai", "a Shōkai door", "compatible with Shōkai v0.3", "passes the Shōkai core conformance suite (v0.3.0)". Name the version and, where it matters, the conformance level or extensions.
- **Talking about the project:** articles, talks, tutorials, comparisons and reviews, including critical ones.
- **Linking** to the specification or this repository with the plain word "Shōkai".
- **Japanese:** 紹介 is an everyday word meaning "introduction". Nothing here limits its ordinary use; this policy is only about using it as the name of this protocol.

## Not allowed

- **Implying endorsement, partnership or approval** by Kigenka or the Shōkai project, or that Kigenka has reviewed your product, unless it has, in writing.
- **"Official" or "approved" claims, or a badge that suggests either.** Shōkai has no badge or approval programme. Passing the conformance runner is something you may state factually (with the version), not an endorsement.
- **Shipping a modified specification or fork under the Shōkai name.** Forks are welcome under the licences, but **a fork must use a different name**. You may say it is "based on Shōkai v0.3.0" or "derived from the Shōkai specification".
- **Using the logo** on your product, packaging, app icon or marketing without written permission. You may show it, unchanged, when writing about the project.
- **Names that could be confused** with Shōkai or Kigenka for agent, identity or introduction products or services (for example a product, domain or package called "Shokai Pro" or "Kigenka Agents").
- **Package names** that suggest an official release, such as anything under the `@kigenka` npm scope or a top-level `shokai` package that isn't published by the project.
