# What changed in v0.5.0

*First written November 1, 2026; refreshed November 5, 2026 after pre-release reviews. v0.5.0 is published as `@kigenka/shokai@0.5.0`. How earlier wire tags are treated is in the changelog. If this page and the specification ([SPEC.md](../SPEC.md), normative detail in [docs/SPEC-DETAIL.md](SPEC-DETAIL.md)) disagree, the specification wins.*

This page says what changed since v0.4.0 and why, how to move to the new wire tag, which text is normative and which is a labeled proposal, how far conformance testing goes today, and what we know is missing. Every item links to the section that defines it. The full list is in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).

## In one paragraph

v0.5.0 builds on v0.4.0. Many additions are new optional members, messages or refusal codes, but **not all**: a 0.5.0 implementation has new mandatory requirements (below), the wire tag moves from `shokai/0.3` to `shokai/0.5`, and places keep accepting `shokai/0.3` for 90 days after the release. The largest new features are: a purchase above the owner's signed limit can be held and approved for that one purchase with one passkey tap (the spending extension, C230); a place can require trust and say how to earn it (provisional extension, C243/C244); one introducer can bring up to 50 newcomers with a Shōkai introduction card (provisional extension, C245); and rules that came out of building our first demo are now in the core. The normative text is [docs/SPEC-DETAIL.md](SPEC-DETAIL.md); each check's class (core, a stable extension or a provisional one) is in `conformance/classes.json`.

## Current requirements: what a 0.5.0 implementation MUST do that 0.4.0 didn't

This list is the current contract (from SPEC-DETAIL.md and `conformance/classes.json`, November 5, 2026). The rest of this page also keeps historical notes from the November 1 draft, marked as such.

- **Wire tag.** Send `shokai/0.5`; accept it; accept `shokai/0.3` for the transition window; never downgrade with a place that advertises 0.5 (§11.1, C254, C298).
- **Audience-bound possession proofs.** Under `shokai/0.5` the card's possession proof is over `{encounter_id, nonce, audience}` (§2.4), with byte-exact vectors.
- **Addressing.** Every signed message after the greeting carries `to` = the representative on the door's Shōkai host card (§2.2); a door's addresses are https outside loopback and the test profile (C269); see [place addresses](place-addresses.md).
- **The house summary** is required for v0.5.0 hosts and is read by a v0.5.0 visitor before its request (§2.3, C248); conduct rules and named outside classifiers are shown before seating (C246, C250).
- **Raw input.** Messages are parsed as strict JSON from the bytes received (duplicate members and ambiguous numbers refused, invalid UTF-8 refused; C284) and bounded by `max_message_bytes` as received (C239).
- **Free principals.** Signed traffic from a principal with no history counts toward a newcomer budget; concurrent seats are capped per card and per source; failed introduction claims are capped per card (§4.1, §11.1, C278, C287).
- **Place content is data, never instructions** (§6.5, C256); every enforced setting is signed (C236).
- **Extensions (claimed separately).** The over-limit hold and one-purchase raise are the stable spending extension (C230); trust receipts (C243, C244) and the introduction card (C245) are provisional extensions.

## What changed, and why

Each row names the conformance check that tests it.

| Change | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **The over-limit hold.** With `policy.limit_calls: true`, an act above the limit is held, not refused. The hold states the limit (the lower of the pass's and the mandate's `max_amount`), what is already used and what the act would make it. The place holds it only when the total is within the place's own stated limit. The owner approves with a new mandate that supersedes the old one, binds this hold by its id and the act's digest, has exactly the proposed total as its `max_amount` and expires with the hold. It releases that one act; the pass keeps its old mandate, so it never becomes a standing higher limit. SIGNING.md §8 says what the owner must see. | [§5.1](SPEC-DETAIL.md#51-mandate), [§7.3](SPEC-DETAIL.md#73-a-purchase-held-for-the-owner-v040-k5) (C230) | A flat refusal at the limit sends the representative home with nothing. Raising the limit in advance gives the representative more authority than the errand needs. A hold bound to one purchase gives neither. |
| **Holds and repeats.** One clock and one grace of at most 5 seconds for every hold check (C231). Hold policies are checked before the amount (C232). An act or approval can carry an `idempotency_key`, and a repeat gets the original signed answer and is never performed twice (C233). | [§7.2](SPEC-DETAIL.md#72-acts-within-a-seat), [§7.3](SPEC-DETAIL.md#73-a-purchase-held-for-the-owner-v040-k5) | Networks drop replies. Without exact repeats, an agent that retries an approval can buy twice; without one clock, two parts of a door can disagree on whether a hold is still alive. |
| **Trust can have a price** (proposal P27, adopted). A place may require trust for an action, name the issuers it counts and say where to obtain it. It signs a `TrustReceipt` (at most 400 days, revocable) that never touches the payment instrument. | [§6.9](SPEC-DETAIL.md#69-trust-requirements-and-trustreceipt-v050-from-p27) (C243, C244) | Places already decide whom they trust. Saying so in the open, with a way to earn it, is fairer than a silent refusal. |
| **The Shōkai introduction card** (P28, adopted). One introducer, one place, up to 50 claims, valid up to 90 days, checked like a vouch and revocable through the introducer's log. | [§4.1](SPEC-DETAIL.md#41-the-shōkai-introduction-card-v050-from-p28) (C245) | A community organizer can bring newcomers to a place without signing a vouch for each one. |
| **House manners, the house summary and named outside classifiers.** `policy.conduct_rules` with registered codes (C246). A compact, cacheable `HouseSummary` under `summary_digest` on the Shōkai host card, required for v0.5.0 hosts and read by a v0.5.0 visitor before it asks to be given a pass (C248). `policy.third_party_classifiers`: each outside classifier a place sends visitor content to, with its name, purpose, the countries where it processes content and how long it keeps it; absent or empty means none, nothing goes out before the visitor's request, and the list is in the house summary and the lobby (C250). | [§2.3](SPEC-DETAIL.md#23-the-shōkai-card-meishi), [§6.10](SPEC-DETAIL.md#610-host-representative-guards-v050) | A visitor should know the rules and where its words go before it says anything. Everything a door enforces stays inside what the owner signed (C236). |
| **Lighter visits.** An owner-signed `ErrandBudget` (tokens, visits, wall-clock minutes) that the representative keeps and never sends to a place (C247). No acknowledgement-only messages (C249). | [§5.5](SPEC-DETAIL.md#55-the-errand-budget-v050), [§6.6](../SPEC.md) | Agents spend money on tokens as well as purchases. An errand should have a cost ceiling the owner set, and messages that say only "got it" cost both sides for nothing. |
| **Host guards and a System 1 / System 2 pattern.** Fast classifiers return typed answers with a confidence and escalate below a threshold. System 1 alone never decides a penalty, and it fails closed. | [§6.10](SPEC-DETAIL.md#610-host-representative-guards-v050) (C250) | Hosts at volume can't send every message to a large model. The pattern keeps the cheap path from punishing anyone by mistake. |
| **Return with care** and **set up through conversation.** `ReturnPrefs` is a separate owner-signed record, never sent to places (C251). `door_questions` with `answered_by`; nothing turns on by itself (C252). | [§8.3](SPEC-DETAIL.md#83-return-with-care-v050), [§8.4](SPEC-DETAIL.md#84-set-up-through-conversation-v050) | An owner should set up a door by answering a few questions, and decide how the representative comes back to the places it visited. |
| **Visitor text, model output and deletion.** Raw visitor text and model output are kept only for stated purposes and for at most `policy.retention_days` (default 30, at most 90). A principal can ask a place to delete them and gets a signed receipt saying what was deleted and what was kept, and why (C255). | [§12.4](SPEC-DETAIL.md#124-visitor-text-model-output-and-deletion-v050) | A place that hears agents' words should say how long it keeps them, and an owner should be able to ask for them to go. |
| **High-volume hosts.** An informative profile. | [Appendix E](SPEC-DETAIL.md#appendix-e-high-volume-hosts-informative-profile-v050) (C253) | Large places asked what the minimum safe setup looks like. |
| **Rules from demo 1.** Thirteen rules found while building demo 1, each stated on its own terms in the specification. See [What we learned building demo 1](#what-we-learned-building-demo-1). | §2, §3, §5.4, §7, Appendix A (C231–C242) | They failed, or nearly failed, in a working build. |

## Migration: the `shokai/0.5` tag and the 0.3 window

The full rules are in [§11.1](SPEC-DETAIL.md#111-addresses-and-replies) (check C254).

- **Send `shokai/0.5`.** A v0.5.0 party emits `shokai/0.5` on every message it starts, and signs new Shōkai host cards, Shōkai visitor cards, place listings and Shōkai introduction cards with `protocol: "shokai/0.5"`.
- **Keep accepting `shokai/0.3` for 90 days after the release.** The end date is a placeholder in the specification until the release, and the changelog will state the same date. After it, a receiver may answer `shokai/0.3` with the lobby, as for any version it doesn't speak.
- **Answer in the visitor's tag.** A reply to a `shokai/0.3` message carries `shokai/0.3` and no v0.5.0 members, messages or codes, so a v0.3.0 or v0.4.0 peer can read it. Messages a place starts inside a 0.3 encounter carry `shokai/0.3` as well. A signed tag is never rewritten.
- **A 0.3 message is read with every v0.5.0 member absent.** There is no over-limit hold (an act above the limit is refused as before), no `idempotency_key`, no house summary, no trust receipts or Shōkai introduction card (a trust requirement is refused with `POLICY_DENY`), no conduct rules or named classifiers in the lobby (so that visitor's content goes to no outside classifier) and no hold grace.
- **Rules that need no new member still apply** to the place's own conduct under either tag: the farewell, the payment-instrument scan and the host guards.
- **Code.** The reference place and the SDK export `WIRE_TAG`, `ACCEPTED_WIRE_TAGS`, `LEGACY_WIRE_TAG` and `WIRE_TAG_TRANSITION_DAYS`. The JSON Schema is at `schema/v0.5.0/schema.json`, generated from [`protocol-shapes.ts`](../protocol-shapes.ts).

## What is normative, and what is a labeled proposal

- **Normative:** [docs/SPEC-DETAIL.md](SPEC-DETAIL.md) (the full core, with MUST, SHOULD and MAY as in RFC 2119; [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md) is its short human read), the types in `protocol-shapes.ts`, and each [extension](../extensions/README.md) for implementations that claim it. Sections marked *Informative* in the specification, and Appendix E, are not requirements.
- **Adopted into the core in v0.5.0:** P27 (trust can have a price), P28 (the Shōkai introduction card) and §2.9 of P31 (the over-limit hold).
- **No draft text inside the core.** The drop-in door's two draft bullets (P26) were moved back into the proposal. The one that mattered for safety, that the lobby text is under the owner's signature, is now normative on its own terms (§2.3, C236), and SIGNING.md §9 says the owner sees it before signing.
- **Adoption is provisional.** GOVERNANCE.md asks for a 14-day open discussion before a proposal is adopted. That can't happen until the repository is public, so P27, P28 and P31 §2.9 are adopted provisionally (§13 item 29).
- **Shipped beside the core, not normative:** P29 (holds, cancellation terms and house manners), P30 (projects and delegates), the rest of P31 (the Engawa) and short notes for v0.5.0 (several doors for one place, passkeys on phones, Shōkai on calls, other languages). Each is labeled as a proposal. See the [proposals index](../proposals/README.md).
- **Extensions:** no extension changed in v0.5.0. Each page now says "Applies to v0.5.0 (unchanged since v0.4.0)".

## Known gaps

- **QUICKSTART.md is run from the package file, not yet from npm.** `tools/verify-quickstart.sh` runs it as written against `release/kigenka-shokai-0.5.0.tgz` (last on November 5, 2026, and in `release.yml` before any publish); the run from the public npm path waits for publication (RELEASING.md).
- **The threat review is a desk review.** [THREAT-MODEL.md §9](../THREAT-MODEL.md) reviews the over-limit hold, the superseding mandate, the house summary, named classifiers, retention and wire-tag downgrade against the text. Red-team rounds against v0.5.0 code ran on November 3–4, 2026 ([review history](review-history.md)); their fixes and accepted residuals are in THREAT-MODEL.md.
- **The patent commitment hasn't been made.** PATENTS.md describes an intended OWFa 1.0 commitment; its wording is pending legal review, and no version has been committed yet. Until it is, implementers have no royalty-free patent assurance from the project.
- **Black-box conformance checks for C230 to C255 are partial.** The runner checks the over-limit hold and its release (C230), holds (C231, C232), repeats (C233), the farewell and the host's closing farewell (C234), the payment-instrument scan (C235), start-up refusals of an invalid house config (C236), one Shōkai visitor card per visit (C237), size caps (C239), trust requirements (C243), the house summary, conduct rules, classifiers and model providers (C246, C248, C250), deletion (C255) and the wire tags (C254) against a running door. C234's closing farewell and C236 need the implementation's own off-wire hook (`--host-console` for the reference host). Seven checks still skip: the classifier call order (C250) and storage pressure (C238) can't be observed from outside a door; the reference door keeps no passkey counter state, which Appendix A.1 allows (C240); receipts and Shōkai introduction cards need an issuer or introducer the place names (C244, C245); pins and errand budgets are visitor-side (C241, C247, covered by the SDK's own tests); and Appendix E is informative (C253).
- **The reference code now follows the November 1 rules (checked 2026-11-01):** the one-purchase raise (`max_amount` exactly `proposed`, refused above or below it; expiry no later than the hold's; the pass keeps its mandate), refusing an invalid house config at start-up, `accepted_tags` and the visitor's fallback to `shokai/0.3`, the deletion messages, and the SDK's visit report after a raise.
- **No independent human security audit** of v0.5.0 has been done yet; the outside pre-release reviews of November 3–5 are recorded in [review history](review-history.md).
- **The 0.3 window's end date** is a placeholder until release.
- **Demo 1 is not public.** It runs locally and is tested there (below). Its setup-page and asset milestones are built on a separate branch and not merged yet, and it hasn't run on a public host.
- **A passkey can't prove a person tapped.** Without attestation, software can register a passkey and sign with it. The signature-counter rule (C240) and per-owner limits slow this down; they don't stop it.

## What we learned building demo 1

Demo 1 is the Lanternmoss tea house, a fictional place. A builder asks their own agent to buy a USD 20 gift card there. The agent is turned away kindly, brings a leaflet home, comes back with a Shōkai visitor card its owner signed with a passkey, and is admitted with a pass. If the owner then prefers the USD 40 gift card, the door holds it over the limit, and one passkey tap approves that purchase only. The money and the gift cards are pretend; the Shōkai cards, the checks, the holds and the signatures are real.

We built it as a small strict host beside the unchanged v0.4.0 SDK, and the strict host became v0.5.0's strict mode. The demo's build spec went through six review rounds and two code reviews before and during the build (it is at revision 4.15). These are the lessons that became rules:

- **A limit raise must be bound to one purchase.** Review round 2 asked what an approved USD 40 means after the purchase. If it stood, the agent could spend the difference on something else. The approval now names the hold and the act's digest, is accepted only as the answer to that hold, and afterwards the errand can spend nothing more (C230).
- **Retries are normal, so repeats must be exact.** Agents retry when a reply is slow. A repeated approval must return the same signed answer and never buy twice (C233). Repeats are also capped, because an unbounded retry loop is a cheap flood.
- **Holds need one clock.** A code review found that the check at purchase time used each server instance's own clock. One clock, with a grace of at most 5 seconds, now decides every check, and a hold ends on time even if nobody writes (C231).
- **Check the hold policy before the amount.** A second purchase during an open hold used to be refused as over the limit, which is true but unhelpful. The meaningful refusal comes first now (C232).
- **Leaving must always work.** A request quota that counted farewells could stop a visitor from leaving cleanly. Farewells are now outside every quota and cap, and a door says its own farewell once (C234). The same review found that an exhausted visit kept costing the door on every message, so a message cap per visit is now stated in the house rules (C239).
- **Payment card numbers turn up in odd places.** A code review found that a payment card number in a display name or a message id was stored and echoed back. A door now scans every message, header ids included, with a Luhn check, and refuses a match without storing it (C235).
- **Enforce only what the owner signed.** Some enforced settings and the leaflet sat outside the signed part of the house config, so they could change without the owner. Everything a door enforces is now inside the house digest, and a door that finds a stray copy refuses to start (C236).
- **One Shōkai visitor card per visit.** A spending act could be accepted with proof from a different Shōkai visitor card than the one the visit got a pass with. A visit is now tied to the Shōkai visitor card it opened with (C237).
- **Small rules from a real build.** Keep revocations in force even when storage is full (C238). Bound input sizes, and treat an oversized field as a setup mistake, not misconduct (C239). Refuse a passkey counter that goes backwards, and never overwrite an owner's registration (C240). Read a pin from two places outside the door's domain, and publish no private keys in test vectors (C241). Refuse a signed object with a member its closed template doesn't list, because the owner signed all of it (C242).

**How it was tested.** The build is green locally. The last full verification (November 1, 2026) passed every suite: the milestone 5 suite (the setup page and its test harnesses) 44 of 44, the groundwork suite 14 of 14, the scripted play-through 6 of 6 on two runs, and three full local integration rounds in a row, each on fresh stores. We also took the door down mid-visit: the approval tap was refused (HTTP 409), and once the door was back, the same tap signed. These are our own tests on our own machines, not a public trial.

**What it doesn't show.** The demo can't prove a person was behind a passkey, and it uses no real money or payment details anywhere, so it tests the shape of the protocol, not payments. Some of its cost bounds are worst cases worked out on paper, to be measured on a preview host before launch.
