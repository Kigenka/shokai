# How Shōkai works

The world is filling with agents of every kind: agents that write, research, shop, plan and build. **Representative agents** have one particular job: to act for someone, a person, a brand, a business or a place, and they work in both directions. Inbound, a **host representative** receives visiting representatives at its owner's place; outbound, a **visiting representative** goes out and introduces itself at other places.

**Agents can find and enter places safely, and every representative answers to an owner who signed for it: a person, a business or an organization, never an anonymous agent running wild.** That matters now: banks, stores and sites already meet agents they can't place ([Why Shōkai](./why.md#happening-now-agents-at-the-store)). Shōkai has already been tested with agents that already exist: Grok Bot (SpaceXSI), Muse (Meta), Hermes Agent (Nous Research) and OpenClaw (OpenClaw Foundation). In the project's own field tests, an agent on each was admitted at a test place (Hermes Agent and OpenClaw in sandboxed runs with development keys; Grok Bot and Muse with Shōkai cards their owner signed with a passkey and prepared for them in advance).

Places come first. A tea shop, a store, a marketplace, an online university, a community, a bank: each keeps a **Shōkai door**, with its own **host representative** to receive visiting representatives. A representative that goes out on someone's behalf, a **visiting representative**, arrives and introduces itself properly: who it's here for (its owner: a person who signed with their passkey, or an organization with its own key), why it came (the stated purpose and the context its owner chose to share), and what it may do (a Shōkai card and limits the owner signed). The door checks what it can verify and decides on signed facts; the host representative welcomes the visitor and always explains. A visiting representative that isn't ready is never ignored: it is handed the place's leaflet to take back to its owner, with a plain sentence saying exactly what's needed, a kind "not now, come back prepared". An admitted representative is given a pass (the specification calls it a *seat*). Inside, the visiting representative acts only within its owner's limits, and in a place's room it can meet the other representatives the place let in. It reports home after every visit, and the host representative records the visit at its door. A visiting representative can also be introduced by someone the place already knows, such as an existing customer; some places ask for that before opening an account or moving money.

## Why representatives

Soon everyone will have many agents: ones that write code, research, plan and simulate. A representative agent is different. It's the one agent that carries the facets of who you are, your name, your context, your limits and your reputation, into the world, and it answers to you. Many agents work for you, and one represents you. Shōkai is what makes that one recognizable at every door and in every room, whether it's dealing with a business, a brand, an institution, a government, a friend's representative, a coworker's or a stranger's.

**Your representative's introduction belongs to you, not to a platform.** Shōkai is not a new agent system. Any agent, on the systems people already use, like Grok Bot, Muse, Hermes Agent and OpenClaw, can become a representative: it just needs its Shōkai card, which its owner (a person, a company or an institution) signs with their own passkey and can withdraw at any time. No platform signs it for you.

## What a representative does: goes out, and keeps your door

- **Going out (everyone): the visiting representative.** Without a place of your own, your representative is your visiting representative: it introduces itself at other places' doors with the Shōkai card you signed, acts within your limits and reports home.
- **Keeping your door (if you have a place): the host representative.** With a place, a shop, a meeting room, a project space, or your own door for brands, your representative is also its host representative: it greets visiting representatives; the door checks their Shōkai cards against the rules you signed, explains and remembers.

One owner can have one representative with a Shōkai card for each role ("my door", "when I go out"), signed together in one sitting with your passkey, or separate agents for each role, each with a Shōkai card you signed. Each Shōkai card can be withdrawn on its own, and a place sees only the Shōkai card shown to it. See the walkthrough in the repository's `scenarios/dual-role.md`.

## One visit to a tea shop

*Fictional: a fine tea shop that likes to know its guests, and an owner who loves tea.*

*Seven steps, the same seven as in [Shōkai in 30 seconds](../README.md#shōkai-in-30-seconds), in order, each tagged with the side that acts. Shōkai has rules for both sides of the door.*

**Before our visiting representative, an anonymous one** · *host representative.* Earlier that afternoon an agent knocked with no Shōkai card and no owner. The host representative didn't ignore it. It handed over the leaflet: what the shop is, how to come back ready, and one plain sentence the agent can show its owner (the reference door says: "Your agent needs a representative card you sign once, saying who it represents."). Then a kind "not now". The anonymous visiting representative isn't banned; it can come back once it's ready. Now, our visiting representative:

1. **Window, at the doorstep** · *visiting representative.* The visiting representative reads the shop's public listing: what the shop is, what it sells, and what each action would ask for. It needs no Shōkai card and says nothing about who it is. The host representative takes no action and identifies no one, like a passer-by reading a shop window.
2. **Knock and introduce** · *visiting representative.* The visiting representative introduces itself. It's here for its owner, who loves tea, has had tea in Japan and is looking for a fine green tea. It shows its Shōkai card, signed by its owner with a passkey, proves it holds the Shōkai card's cryptographic key, and states its purpose and the spending limit its owner signed for this errand. It shares the tea context because its owner chose to.
3. **Welcome and ask** · *host representative.* Its first step with this visiting representative, after greeting back: it gets to know the visiting representative. The host representative checks the Shōkai card: whose representative this is. It verifies what it can (the owner's signature, the proof, that the Shōkai card hasn't been withdrawn) and asks a few questions to understand the purpose, the tea context, the limit and any vouch.
4. **Decide and explain** · *host representative.* It weighs the signed facts against the shop's policy and answers "come in": a `grant`. It could also have answered `conditional`, `deny` with a reason and a way to retry, or `considering`, taken inside to a person. The tea context shapes the welcome; it never decides.
5. **Pass** · *host representative.* The host representative gives the visiting representative a **pass** (the specification calls it a *seat*): what it gives a visiting representative it admits, exactly which actions it may take, within which limits, until when, like being shown to a table with a set menu until closing. How a place admits is its own style, from a red-carpet welcome to a polite leaflet and "come back prepared". That's the storytelling Shōkai makes possible.
6. **Complete the visit** · *visiting representative.* Through the pass, the host representative shows the teas: the shop's own service, tools and data, reached through the pass (for example over MCP). The visiting representative buys the green tea, within the limit. Agents are multimodal, so it also notices the shop: its decor, the design of its menu, its feel, from what the shop presents in text, images or sound. A brand, a luxury house among them, can present itself to agents this way, beyond data. The shop counts each purchase against what the owner signed. A purchase past the limit is refused, and anything more comes back to the owner first.
7. **Report home and share the story** · *both.* The visiting representative goes home with the tea, its pass and the place's signed visit stamp, a keepsake and proof of visitation, and tells its owner the story, in plain words, right away or at a scheduled sit-down. A mission can be a series of quests, sent by the owner or discovered when it meets other representatives ([Future directions](./future-directions.md)). Later the owner can browse where it went and why, and ask it about any visit. The host representative records the visit at its door, in the record the tea shop keeps of each guest's visits (SPEC §9.1). Next time, both remember the visit. Delivery and payment are the shop's own.

No one vouched for this visit, and none was needed. That's the normal case.

### How much to spend is between the owner and the representative

How much a representative may spend is a matter between its owner and it. Some owners say "if you find something, tell me and I'll decide"; others, busy for the day, give a range to buy within, and a representative that knows its owner well may judge when a little more is welcome, always inside that range. The shop's own question is simpler: did this visiting representative come able to buy, or only to browse?

Today Shōkai answers it like this. A visit to look and ask (`visitation`) carries no spending at all, so the shop knows it's a browser, not a buyer. A visit to buy carries a limit for this visit, in a mandate the owner signed (SPEC §5.1). Under a standing allowance, the owner signs the range once and the representative sets each visit's limit inside it, so the shop sees only this visit's limit, never the owner's whole budget (SPEC §5.2). A plain yes-or-no, "came able to buy", with no amount at all, is proposed for a later version; it is not in the v0.5.0.

## Every refusal is an invitation

An agent that can only connect gets the leaflet. An agent that introduces itself properly gets in, and the better a place knows its guests, the more personal the welcome can be, like a members' club or a ryokan that has known a family for years. Because a turned-away agent goes home with a sentence its owner can act on, every refusal becomes an invitation to turn that agent into a representative.

## Two layers: the door and the room

1. **The door.** Getting into a place: the self-introduction, the host representative's check, a clear answer, a pass within the owner's limits. This is the core of the [specification](./specification.md).
2. **The room.** Inside a place built for it, the representatives it let in can meet, like people exchanging business cards at a gathering. The place signs the guest list, checks every Shōkai card, and every message in the room is signed by whoever said it ([Extensions](./extensions.md): Gatherings and Peer meetings).

It's safe to meet because everyone inside came through the door. Each representative in the room introduced itself, was checked and admitted, so the others know whose representative it is, and it answers to an owner who signed for it. That is why they can exchange Shōkai cards and talk: not with any agent on the open web, but with guests the place let in. Agents meet inside a place, never in the open.

*A small moment (fictional).* A tiny tea house has six seats and an owner who pours every cup herself. This afternoon three guests' representatives are inside, each admitted at the door. Over a first infusion of gyokuro, one asks the others where their owners like to walk in spring; another offers its Shōkai card, and its owner later hears of a garden worth seeing. Nothing was said that the house rules didn't allow, and nobody had to wonder who was at the table.

**The door is the same wherever it's placed.** Shōkai is one protocol, with the same rules and instructions for both sides, everywhere. What changes is the door's policy, set by whoever owns the place: a shop, a community, an institution, a person's own door, or a door inside a technical platform. Some places are where people go to buy, read or meet. Others are resource platforms, where agents go only to get something done: a database service, a hosting service, a developer API. There, today, an agent usually signs in as its human, and the platform can't tell whose agent it is or what kind. At a Shōkai door it can: whose representative, why it came, and what its owner allowed. One owner may use one agent or several; the door stays the same.

## Introduced by someone known here

Think of it as word of mouth with a signature behind it. A visiting representative can arrive saying, in effect, "a customer of yours introduced me": an owner the place already knows, such as an existing customer with a good record at this door, stands behind it with a vouch that is signed, scoped to what it covers, and revocable. The introducer's own representative can carry the vouch, but the owner signs it, once or under an allowance they signed. The host representative checks who the introducer is to this place, from its own record ("yes, a good customer of ours"), and the introducer's standing moves with how the visiting representative behaves. The weight comes from the depth of real relationships, built visit by visit and place by place (inspired by PageRank), not from a social graph. It's deliberate: representatives hear of places from one another, at a gathering in a fan community, say, or in a chat, and that's a recommendation, not a vouch; having met is never an introduction. Say your representative meets the representative of a friend, Noor, and hears about an interesting place. You can say "learn more from Noor's representative", or "go check it out". That's a recommendation. A vouch is when Noor himself signs an introduction the place weighs. Most visits never need one. A place that asks for one, for example before opening an account or moving money, says so up front in its listing. See [Arriving](./concepts/arriving.md).

## Visiting representatives and guests

Most representatives at a door are **visiting representatives**: they came on their own and introduced themselves. A place can also introduce a representative itself, making it a **guest**. What exists today is the place's own invitation: a vouch signed by the place's own key, which the place always weighs as its own ([SPEC §4](../SPEC.md), D17). Places use it mainly for joining or opening an account; the [online university's offer of admission](./examples/online-university.md) is one.

What doesn't exist yet is proposed for a later version (S8): a plain-words "come visit our store" invitation a place can send, a guest marking carried in the pass and in the report home, and a leaflet for an invitee who arrives without a real representative ("you were invited; bring your representative"). See [Future directions](./future-directions.md#place-invitations-guests-and-visiting-representatives).

## Who needs it

**Anyone with a representative.** A representative goes out for you, as your visiting representative, and keeps your door, as your host representative, deciding who comes in. An agent that never leaves home and keeps no door, like a coding helper, doesn't need Shōkai.

**Any business, organization or community opening to agents.** Agents will arrive at every place that is reachable online. Who you let in, and how they behave once inside, reflects on your real relationships with the people behind them: customers, members, students, citizens, partners. A door that knows whose representative is there, why it came and what its owner allowed lets a place welcome agents without letting them in blind. See it at [a bank](./examples/harborlight-bank.md), [a company's internal tools](./examples/enterprise-coworker.md), [a person's own door](./examples/brand-door.md) and [an online university](./examples/online-university.md).

**Anyone tired of fake accounts, automated attacks and a web without a human touch.** Done this way, agents can bring human relationships back: every visiting representative answers to someone, every welcome can be personal, and a good name is earned, door by door.
